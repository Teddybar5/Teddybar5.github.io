Overview

March–August 2025 · Unreal Engine 5

Obedience Experiment is a first-person, 3D psychological horror puzzle game in which players must fight the system in order to succeed. Players assume the role of a Patriot Test administrator in a fictional authoritarian state, tasked with determining whether citizens are loyal or if they need to be motivated through a brutal electric shock. Originally developed for a horror games course, the project has continued beyond the class. Since the class ended I have been the sole developer on this project and pushed it forward for a steam release.

The game is currently available on steam now!

Content Design

All of the creative decisions about the gameplay and vision of the game were made by me, along with the initial idea for the game. At first I wanted the game to follow the real life Milgram Experiment pretty faithfully, and attempt to subtly encourage players to stop shocking the npcs. This vision eventually shifted after the first rounds of playtesting because players who decided not to shock npcs would end up with an unengaging experience that felt more like a movie than a game. That is why I changed the game to be a simulationb> that the player is within, and that they are truly the one being tested, not the npcs.

This also made the game far more engaging by creating a mystery that evolves throughout the game, this mystery is also the way for the player to access the secret ending. There are 3 total endings, the secret one I just mentioned, the bad ending where players obey everything, and the monster ending where the player disobeys too much and gets caught by the dystopian country.

A lot of care went in to ensure that everything felt like it could realistically exist within this fake future dystopian country, which was a big reason why I changed it from taking place in real life to taking place within a simulation. It never really made any sense that they would pick a random citizen to administer a test to other civilians, it made more sense for the administration of the test to actually be the real test, and to uncover citizens that showed empathy and consideration for their peers.

Level Design

I designed and implemented the entire environment and lighting. The setting was inspired by Soviet-era scientific facilities, using curated assets alongside custom props created by a team artist, which I integrated and refined in-engine. I received assets from the artists on my team and implemented them myself, adjusting materials and lighting to ensure visual cohesion.

Animation and lighting played a critical role in curating the atmosphere. Rather than showing NPC entrances directly, I implemented heavy steel doors to conceal movement and heighten tension. I collaborated with another designer on NPC animations during questioning and punishment sequences. Lighting was carefully composed to focus attention on the test subject, using a single overhead spotlight, while darker corners of the room emphasize the presence of the emerging entity.

Production

As team lead I was in charge of managing a team of 5 others to get the project finished on time. We had a short deadline of 2 months from when we started the project since it was due at the end of the class. I used a google doc to manage tasks and held weekly meetings to go over progress updates and delegate upcoming tasks. Rather than push the team using sprints and a more structured development process, I worked around everyone’s busy schedules and if one person had a particularly busy week their workload would be divided between me and anyone else with lighter weeks. This allowed us to stay on track and get a polished build finished by the end of the class that was ready to present and submit.

Since them I have been working as a solo developer on this project to push it across the finsih line. I have learned a lot about not just design and development, but about production, building the project, and even marking like making trailers and content to promote my project.

UI

I created all of the UI and menus for the game. I did not want a HUD or any intrusive UI, so I made the UI exist in world space to increase immersion. When you interact with the TV it rolls up towards you and displays the pause menu. Here you can access the “Secret Channels” that allow you to escape the simulation through the secret ending. I also created the main menu to feel simple and imply that you are entering a simulation.

Live Action Videos

I wrote, directed, edited, and even acted in 2 live-action videos within the game. The first was the intro cutscene. I have always loved the Fallout 4 SPECIAL videos that describe parts of the game, and I wanted to make a video like that for this game that would describe the test you are administering and provide some more information about the game’s world. The intro video is in black and white with silent film text cards to emulate an informational video from the 40s or 50s, because that is the technology era that the game’s world exists in.

I also created another live-action video within the game. This video is a transmission from revolutionaries that have hacked into the simulation that the player is stuck inside. They beg you to escape and give you some tools to aid in your escape. I wanted this video to feel like it was recorded on an old camcorder and transmitted into the simulation. Both these videos were a lot of fun to direct and implement and they added a lot of charm to the project.
